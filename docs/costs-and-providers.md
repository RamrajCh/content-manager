Official-source provider research, free-tier constraints, illustrative capacity/cost assessment, and adoption checklist. Research date and uncertainty are preserved; deployment selection remains deferred.

## 8. Cost, free tiers, and Nepal availability assessment

### 8.1 Research method and confidence

Facts below were read from official sources on 2026-10-26. A published allowance is not a guaranteed permanent offer. "Verified" refers to the cited page, not a newly created account, provider support confirmation, Nepal billing eligibility, real network latency, or inbox-delivery trial. No external accounts were opened, credentials submitted, payments made, or deployments executed.

Nepal suitability has three independent dimensions: owner signup/billing eligibility; nearby service region and latency; and receiving payment/email availability. These must not be conflated. Official material reviewed does **not** establish Nepal owner eligibility for every candidate. Marking those items unverified is deliberate, not a claim they are unsupported.

### 8.3 Illustrative capacity and cost model

This is a scenario, not a demand forecast or price guarantee:

- 40 files at 100MB each = 4GB live assets; a second independent copy = another 4GB, leaving approximately 2GB of a 10GB-month allowance for quarantine, retained versions, covers and DB backup exports **if stored within that same billing allowance**. This is tight; cross-account backup may have separate billing and access policies. Do not treat duplicate objects in one writable bucket as independent recovery.
- One 4GB catalog download by 250 customers implies about 1TB transferred if each downloads the full catalog. R2 states free internet egress; API signing, operation counts, email, host bandwidth for public pages, and abuse still matter. Route file bytes directly from storage, not through API host.
- 60 access emails/day + 10 receipts/recovery emails/day = 70/day and about 2,100 over 30 days; below Resend's listed 100/day and 3,000/month. A burst of 120 in one day exceeds daily quota even if the month is below 3,000.
- A database export of 50MB/day retained as 7 dailies +4 weeklies can consume about 550MB before compression, plus manifest/log assets. At 0.5GB database size the equivalent copies approach 5.5GB, so retention can exceed the storage allowance long before catalog growth.
- Monthly estimate formula: eligible compute + requests + storage GB-month + operation units + paid egress where applicable + email + logs/monitoring + backups + domain/12 + taxes/payment FX/fees if applicable. Quote current eligible prices only after selecting providers and accounts.

**Conclusion:** Some usage may fit free allowances, but a complete permanently free, commercially eligible, Nepal-onboarded production deployment is **not verified**. Initial preference for free services and deferred deployment decisions are preserved. Before launch, either demonstrate all gates within eligible free services or explicitly accept an affordable paid upgrade; never silently degrade access correctness or recovery to achieve "free."

### 8.4 Provider adoption checklist

For each candidate, owner records: current plan/terms URL and date; commercial-use permission; Nepal signup and identity verification eligibility; billing instrument/currency/tax support; actual selected region/latency; data-processing/retention terms; quota/reset/overage/suspension behavior; credentials/MFA; export/restore proof; worker scheduling; support path. Verify signup with provider documentation/support and owner-controlled account only at deployment time. Do not infer availability from region maps. For QR account additionally verify merchant/charitable/support use permission, NPR precision/limits, credited net/gross presentation and unique transaction reference visibility.
